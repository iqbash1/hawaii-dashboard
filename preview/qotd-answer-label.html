QOTD answer — does a metric label fix the missing context?

The current answer reveal drops a bare number ("In 2024, Hawaiʻi was -64.6 per 10K versus the median of 6.2 per 10K") with no metric name, no unit translation, no direction. Testing whether prepending the metric as a small pill above the answer is enough — across three different unit shapes (signed rate, ¢/kWh, ratio). A third panel rewrites the sentence so the metric is the subject, for comparison.

Case 1 — Net migration V1 · signed · per 10K

Now

Do you know Hawaiʻi?

More people move to Hawaiʻi than leave.

✗ Not quite.

Answer: False

In 2024, Hawaiʻi was -64.6 per 10K versus the median of 6.2 per 10K.

With metric label

Do you know Hawaiʻi?

More people move to Hawaiʻi than leave.

✗ Not quite.

Answer: False

Net Migration

In 2024, Hawaiʻi was -64.6 per 10K versus the median of 6.2 per 10K.

Rewritten (metric as subject)

Do you know Hawaiʻi?

More people move to Hawaiʻi than leave.

✗ Not quite.

Answer: False

In 2024, Hawaiʻi lost about 65 residents per 10,000 to other states, while most states gained residents.

Read: the label tells you what metric it is, but "-64.6 per 10K" still reads as jargon and the negative sign reads as a typo. The rewrite folds metric + unit + direction into one readable sentence.

Case 2 — Residential electricity price V1 · ¢/kWh

Now

Do you know Hawaiʻi?

Hawaiʻi has higher electricity prices than most states.

✓ You got it!

Answer: True

In 2025, Hawaiʻi was 40.6¢ versus the median of 15.3¢.

With metric label

Do you know Hawaiʻi?

Hawaiʻi has higher electricity prices than most states.

✓ You got it!

Answer: True

Residential Electricity Price

In 2025, Hawaiʻi was 40.6¢ versus the median of 15.3¢.

Rewritten (metric as subject)

Do you know Hawaiʻi?

Hawaiʻi has higher electricity prices than most states.

✓ You got it!

Answer: True

In 2025, Hawaiʻi residents paid 40.6¢ per kWh of electricity — about 2.7× the 15.3¢ most states pay.

Read: the label here is mostly redundant — "electricity prices" already lives in the claim. "40.6¢" still floats without "/kWh"; the rewrite restores the unit and the 2.7× ratio gives the size of the gap.

Case 3 — Home price-to-income ratio V1 · ratio · 8.7x

Now

Do you know Hawaiʻi?

Hawaiʻi homes cost a bigger share of income than most states.

✓ You got it!

Answer: True

In 2024, Hawaiʻi was 8.7x versus the median of 4.2x.

With metric label

Do you know Hawaiʻi?

Hawaiʻi homes cost a bigger share of income than most states.

✓ You got it!

Answer: True

Home Price-to-Income Ratio

In 2024, Hawaiʻi was 8.7x versus the median of 4.2x.

Rewritten (metric as subject)

Do you know Hawaiʻi?

Hawaiʻi homes cost a bigger share of income than most states.

✓ You got it!

Answer: True

In 2024, the typical Hawaiʻi home cost 8.7 years of the typical household's income, versus 4.2 years in most states.

Read: the label names the metric but "8.7x" still requires the reader to remember the formula. The rewrite reframes "x" as "years of income" — same math, immediate meaning.
Verdict to discuss: a metric label is a useful safety net for the few answers where the claim doesn't already name the metric — but it doesn't fix unit jargon, signed values, or "median" framing. For the 25 V1/V2 answers, rewriting the sentence with the metric as subject does both jobs in one move.
