29 August 2026

Homelessness is more about housing costs than income.

Adjusting for cost of living, Alabama’s income per person is about the same as Hawaiʻi’s, yet in 2024 it had about 5 unsheltered people per 10,000 residents against Hawaiʻi’s 28. The five priciest housing markets all rank among the ten worst for homelessness. Homelessness follows housing costs, not paychecks.

19 August 2026

Easy to vote. Less to vote for.

Hawaiʻi is the 4th-easiest state in America to cast a ballot and ranks #50 on presidential turnout. All-mail voting did lift turnout, by about 7 points between 2016 and 2024, more than double the average in states without it. Oregon has voted by mail since 2000 and ranks #7; Hawaiʻi started in 2020. What changed instead is the ballot, with fewer candidates running.

Common incomes. Uncommon costs.

Hawaiʻi looks equal on income, ranking 12th best on the Gini index and holding near the top for most of two decades. But that small gap comes with modest incomes: South Dakota and Nebraska are just as equal and far richer, and Hawaiʻi is the most expensive state for a resident to buy a home.

29 May 2026

A safe state with a theft problem.

Hawaiʻi is one of the safest states from violent crime but ranks #40 of 50 on property crime, and has almost always been both since 1960. The difference is due to tourism: opportunistic theft clusters where visitors do, in Waikīkī and the resort corridors.

Florida has Hawaiʻi beat on rent burden.

Hawaiʻi has ranked last on home price-to-income every year since 2008. On rent burden, it ranks #47, with Florida holding the worst spot. Two housing problems, not one, and supply alone won’t fix both.

Hawaiʻi has the rainy day fund, finally.

Hawaiʻi’s rainy day fund crossed the GFOA-recommended 10% floor in 2024 and held in 2025, the first time on record. The fund was built by vote, not by rule, and a vote can drain it.

17 May 2026

Seventeen years of low violent crime with high homelessness.

Every year since HUD began counting unsheltered homelessness in 2007, Hawaiʻi has ranked in the top tier of states on that metric while staying safer than the national median on violent crime. No other state has matched the combination every year. Oregon came closest at 16 of 17; Washington at 14.

12 May 2026

One of America’s tightest labor markets, with the weakest productivity growth.

Hawaiʻi has one of the tightest labor markets in the country, but its productivity growth has almost stalled. From 2018 to 2024, Hawaiʻi fell from #7 to #41 on the BLS productivity index while the US median grew 11%.

How Hawaiʻi rose in 8th-grade reading without raising its score.

Hawaiʻi’s 8th-grade NAEP reading rank climbed from #45 to #26 while the score barely moved. The country fell to where Hawaiʻi was; Hawaiʻi was one of only three states whose score didn’t fall during COVID.

Hawaiʻi quadrupled its renewables. The energy prices got worse.

From 2003 to 2024, Hawaiʻi’s renewable share quadrupled and the residential electricity price gap with the mainland more than tripled. A smaller ratepayer base is carrying both the legacy oil system and the clean-energy buildout.
