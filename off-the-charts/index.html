Off the Charts

5 May 2026

Every expensive state in America is rich. Not Hawaiʻi.

Look at the five most unaffordable states for housing in 2024 (years of full income to buy a median home, per home price-to-income ratio):

StateHPIIncome rank
Hawaiʻi8.7#46
California7.6#12
Washington6.1#9
Colorado5.9#7
Massachusetts5.8#3

Four of those five carry the housing premium on top-12 incomes. They’re expensive places where residents earn enough to absorb the cost. Hawaiʻi is the outlier: highest housing burden in the country, with real per capita income of just $52,272, fifth-from-bottom nationally.

This is the math behind the migration. People can stomach being expensive if they’re well-paid for it. People can stomach being underpaid if their cost of living is low. Hawaiʻi asks them to accept both, simultaneously, and Hawaiʻi’s #50 net migration rank shows what happens when they decline.

Every “high-cost peer” comparison flatters us. We’re the only state in America where the cost has decoupled from the wage entirely.
