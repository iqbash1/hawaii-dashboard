Off the Charts

19 August 2026

Easy to vote. Less to vote for.

The 2024 Cost of Voting Index ranks Hawaiʻi the 4th-easiest state in America to cast a ballot. All-mail since 2020: a ballot mailed to every registered voter.

Hawaiʻi’s presidential turnout ranks #50. So says the U.S. Elections Project, the national turnout count.

Turnout did rise after the reform: about 7 points since 2016, from 43% to 50%. States without all-mail averaged about 3; Hawaiʻi more than doubled it.

The rank did not move: #50 in five of the past six presidential cycles. Oregon, all-mail since 2000, is #7; Colorado, since 2013, #6. Hawaiʻi reformed late.

Last place is not a technicality. Neither Oregon nor Hawaiʻi has voted Republican for president since 1984; one still turns out. Strike all 43,000 stationed service members from the eligible-voter count and assume all voted elsewhere: still last.

What changed is what’s on the ballot. 279 candidates filed statewide in 2024, a decade low. Sixteen of 51 state House seats drew no challenger. The ballot is easy to get; what’s on it is getting easy to ignore.

The August 2026 primary re-ran the experiment. Early returns on Oʻahu, with no marquee race, lagged the last two primaries; on Kauaʻi and Maui, with contested mayor’s races, at or ahead of pace. Same mail system everywhere. “Competition in elections drives turnout,” University of Hawaiʻi political scientist Colin Moore said.

Mail voting did what mail voting can do. The remaining gap to Colorado and Oregon is not about how the ballot arrives; it’s about what’s on it.
