Off the Charts

17 May 2026

Ten years of low violent crime with high homelessness.

Every year of the past decade, Hawaiʻi has ranked in the top 10 nationally for unsheltered homelessness while remaining in the safer half of the country on violent crime. No other state has matched that combination every year of the decade.

Oregon came closest, holding the same position 9 of 10 years. In 2024, Oregon’s violent crime sat above the national median, ending its streak. Washington held the corner 7 years before exiting in 2022. Hawaiʻi alone has stayed.

The pattern matters because the dominant public framing assumes the two metrics move together. They don’t. Hawaiʻi’s unsheltered rate rose 30% over the decade. Its violent crime fell 11%. Oregon’s homelessness also rose, but its violent crime climbed 47%. The same regional pressure produced opposite outcomes on safety.

Hawaiʻi’s policy conversation regularly conflates visible homelessness with public safety. A decade of data says they are different problems requiring different answers.
