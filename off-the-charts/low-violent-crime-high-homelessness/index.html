Off the Charts

17 May 2026

Seventeen years of low violent crime with high homelessness.

Every year since HUD began counting unsheltered homelessness in 2007, Hawaiʻi has ranked in the bottom tier of states on that metric while remaining safer than the national median on violent crime (top tier in most years). No other state has matched that combination every year of the seventeen-year window.

Oregon came closest at 16 of 17 years; in 2024 its violent crime crossed above the national median, ending its streak. Washington held the combination for 14 years before exiting in 2022. California has been bottom tier on homelessness throughout, but its violent crime never met the median bar. Hawaiʻi alone has stayed.

A state counts in a year if both were true: it ranked in the worst third nationally on unsheltered homelessness, and it had less violent crime than half the country. 17 years total (2007–2024; 2021’s federal homelessness count was paused).

Hawaiʻi’s unsheltered rate rose 10% since 2007; its violent crime fell 17%. Oregon’s homelessness rose 34%, but its violent crime climbed 16%. The same regional pressure produced opposite outcomes on safety.

Public debate in Hawaiʻi often pairs visible homelessness with violent crime. State-level data over seventeen years show they don’t move together.
